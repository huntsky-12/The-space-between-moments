THE SPACE BETWEEN MOMENTS.
Chapter II

Maybe It Wasn't Love

I never told her.
Not because I didn't want to.
God, I wanted to.
Begin reading ↓
I — The Words I Never Said

So I said nothing.

I never told her.
Not because I didn't want to.

God, I wanted to....

There were so many nights when I would lie awake and rehearse the words in my head....

Different versions
of the same confession, each one sounding more ridiculous than the last.

I like you.
Too simple.
I think I've fallen for you.
Too much.
I don't know what this is, but I know I don't want to lose you.
Too honest.
So I said nothing.

Maybe silence was easier.
At least silence couldn't embarrass me.
It couldn't look at me with pity.
It couldn't say, I'm sorry,
I don't feel the same way.
And it certainly couldn't change the way she smiled at me the next morning.

But there was another reason I never told her. I was afraid of myself.
Afraid that perhaps what
I felt wasn't love at all....

Perhaps it was just attraction dressed up in prettier words because I wanted it to mean something more....

Perhaps I didn't love her....

II — Afraid of Myself

Perhaps I didn't love her.....

Perhaps I loved the way she made me feel....

And what kind of person would I be if I mistook wanting someone for loving them?

So I watched.

I watched the way she tucked her hair behind her ear when she was concentrating. The way she laughed a
little louder when she was genuinely happy. The way she remembered tiny things,
people told her and
forgot the important things about herself.

I noticed when she was tired.
When she was pretending not to be upset.
When she needed someone to sit beside her but didn't know how to ask.

And slowly, I began to wonder if lust could really be this patient.

Because I didn't just want to hold her.
Sometimes I wanted to hold the silence around her.
I wanted to sit beside her on the days when she had nothing interesting to say.

I wanted to know what frightened her.
What she regretted.
What she dreamed about when nobody was listening.

I wanted to see her succeed even if I wasn't standing anywhere near the picture.

But there was something else I never told anyone.

I had started living with her in my dreams.
Not the kind of dreams that made me wake up embarrassed.
The quieter ones.
The dangerous ones.
The ones that felt so ordinary that, for a few seconds after waking, I forgot they weren't real.

Sometimes I dreamed that we were together...

.

III — The Quiet Dreams

I had started living with her in my dreams.

We would be walking somewhere with no particular destination, arguing about something stupid...

She would laugh, and I would look at her the way I always wanted to but never allowed myself to....

Sometimes we were sitting in a room late at night, talking about everything and nothing.

Sometimes she would fall asleep beside me while I was still awake.

Sometimes there wasn't even a story.

Just her presence....

Just the strange comfort of knowing that, somehow, she was mine and I was hers.

And those dreams made me happier than they had any right to.

I would wake up with a smile.
Then reality would arrive slowly.
The empty space beside me.
The ceiling above my head.

The realization that I had spent another night living a life I was too afraid to ask for...

And for a moment, I would lie there wishing I could go back to sleep....

Not because I wanted to escape reality.
But because, for a few hours, reality had been kinder to me....

So I started manifesting us...

At first, I didn't even realize I was doing it.

I would imagine her sitting beside me.
Imagine introducing her to people.
Imagine stupid little conversations.
Imagine telling her about my day.
Imagine her telling me about hers.

I imagined us growing comfortable with each other the kind of comfort where silence didn't need to be
filled.

IV — Manifesting Us

I started manifesting us.

I imagined reaching for her hand without wondering whether I was allowed to.

I imagined the first time she would look at me and I wouldn't have to wonder what she meant.

I imagined a future that had never been promised to me.

And every time I imagined it, I knew I was walking a dangerous line.

Because there is something beautiful about imagining a person you love beside you.

But there is also something cruel about teaching your heart to expect someone who has never said they
are coming.

Still, I couldn't stop.

Maybe because those little imaginary moments were the closest I had to knowing what happiness with her
might feel like.

And perhaps that was the part that frightened me most.

I wasn't imagining kissing her all the time.
I wasn't imagining anything physical.

I was imagining life.
Her complaining about something trivial.
Me remembering how she takes her coffee.
Her sending me a message because something reminded her of me.
Falling asleep after talking for hours.
Waking up knowing she was still there.
Growing older without having to wonder whether she would disappear from my life.

And perhaps that was when I began to understand something.

Maybe love isn't always about wanting someone to be yours.
Maybe sometimes it's about imagining the smallest, most ordinary pieces of a life with them.
The grocery trips.
The arguments.

V — Imagining Life

I was imagining life.

The bad days.
The boring afternoons.
The comfortable silences.
The moments that nobody writes poems about.

And wanting all of them.
Not because they are perfect.
But because they are with her.

I began to wonder if lust could really make someone want the boring parts too.
Could lust make me care about the things that had nothing to do with her body?
Could it make me happy simply because she was happy?
Could it make me imagine growing old with someone I hadn't even held?

I didn't know.

And I was still afraid to call it love.

Because calling it love made it real.
And if it was real, then eventually I would have to do something with it.

Eventually, I would have to tell her.
And that meant risking the one thing I had managed to protect all this time
the possibility of us.

Because as long as I said nothing,
us could exist somewhere.
In my dreams....
In my thoughts....
In the little manifestations I made before falling asleep....
In the imaginary future I visited whenever I missed her....

She could still be mine there....

.

VI — The Possibility of Us

The possibility of us....

And I could still be hers.
No rejection.
No awkward silence.
No I don't feel the same way.

Just us....
Exactly the way I wanted us to be.

But maybe that wasn't fair.
Because she wasn't a character in my dreams.
She was a person.
She had her own heart.
Her own wishes.
Her own right to choose whether there would ever be an us.

And perhaps loving someone meant accepting that their choice mattered more than the story I had written
about them in my head.

So I kept my dreams to myself.
I kept my manifestations quiet.
I kept the imaginary version of us somewhere no one could touch.

And every morning, I returned to reality.
Still afraid...
Still wondering....
Still asking myself whether what I felt was love or merely a beautiful form of wanting...

But one thing had begun to change.
I no longer feared that I would stop loving her if I discovered it was only lust.
I feared something else.

VII — Her Choice

She was a person.

I feared, That I would never tell her.
That I would spend years imagining a life with her while she lived an entirely different one.
That one day, I would look back and realize I had protected myself so carefully from rejection that I had
accidentally protected myself from happiness too.

VIII — Still...

And in the end....

Still...

I never told her.

Not yet.

But that night, before I fell asleep,
I closed my eyes and imagined her beside me again.

She was laughing.
I was pretending to be annoyed.

Nothing extraordinary was happening.

And somehow, it was the happiest I had ever been.

For once, I didn't try to imagine the ending.
I just stayed there.
With her.

For as long as the dream would let me.
End of Chapter II
